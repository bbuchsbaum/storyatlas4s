# Vision

storyatlas4s aims to make a narrative—and the ways people reconstruct it—
explorable without turning either into a misleading picture.

The destination is a coordinated scientific instrument for moving from an
exact word in a story or transcript to propositions, events, scenes, episodes,
relations, feature trajectories, and recall mappings, then back to the words
and evidence that justify them. It should work for a short story, a novel, a
known-source recall, and an autobiographical interview in which no independent
source event exists.

The instrument is not one master diagram and not a gallery of unrelated views.
It is one research workspace with:

- a docked **Narrative Codex** containing the exact observational text;
- one replaceable projection for the question at hand;
- a persistent inspector for evidence, alternatives, uncertainty, and
  derivation;
- shared semantic focus and selection, with explicit packet-specific evidence
  horizons, relation filters, and feature choices.

Without recall, the natural first view is the Codex beside a **Discourse
Atlas** showing how the telling unfolds. With a known-source recall, the
workspace opens as source and recall together, with an alignment matrix as the
most honest first account of posterior mass. Other projections reveal
story-world chronology, relations, numerical feature scale-space, or the route
taken through the source. Changing projection should change the question, not
lose the object being investigated.

## An evidence-preserving way to see

The exact text is the observational coordinate system. A researcher should be
able to follow a disciplined loop:

1. notice a pattern in an overview;
2. select the event, entity, relation, feature observation, or recall unit;
3. reach the exact supporting words;
4. inspect provenance, uncertainty, alternatives, and the layout rule;
5. change scale or projection while preserving semantic focus.

Every interpretive mark must participate in that loop. A mark that cannot be
resolved to evidence is decoration or debt.

The instrument must keep unlike coordinates unlike. Source presentation order,
story-world temporal constraints, recall or interview time, and analytical
scale are not one axis. A flashback, a retrospective mention, and a recall
backtrack may look similar in prose but mean different things. Incomparable
world-time events must remain incomparable; a layout must not give them false
durations or a false chronology merely because a drawing needs positions.

Uncertainty must be structure, not atmosphere. Missing data is not zero. Low
coverage is not low magnitude. A raw score is not a calibrated probability.
An alternative interpretation, unresolved claim, excluded source, distorted
detail, and external association each require a distinct and inspectable
state. Visual smoothness must never be purchased by silently dropping mass or
manufacturing precision.

## From word to novel

At word and sentence scale, the Codex should support ordinary reading while
showing local entities, propositions, contexts, references, relations, and
feature tracks. A word-level measure such as imageability should be viewable as
raw observations, as a declared reduction over windows, and as an aggregate at
event, scene, or episode scale. Units, reducer, window basis, coverage,
missingness, and derivation must travel with every displayed value.

At story and novel scale, the same semantic identities must survive. Semantic
zoom is a focus-and-context transition, not optical magnification. It moves
over two independent axes—what narrative object is represented and how much
surface structure is shown—while feature scale remains a separate measurement
choice. A continuous gesture may select among discrete, receipted semantic
states, but screen density is only a presentation policy. The Codex never
replaces exact words with a summary.

Large works require quiet overviews, stable breadcrumbs, search, bounded mark
budgets, and layout-only aggregation with a deterministic path back to every
hidden member. A selected event may become visible through an ancestor when
zoomed out and return to its own mark when zoomed in. Its semantic address and
textual focus must not disappear in between.

## Recall as a route through remembered structure

For a known source, the first faithful mapping view is an alignment matrix:
recall units in recall order against source nodes or support, with hierarchy,
posterior spread, distorted facets, external states, abstention, and unresolved
mass kept visible. Authorized dual text rails support close adjudication.
Routes over the Discourse Atlas or a chronology projection reveal jumps,
returns, compression, dwell, and backtracking without concealing multimodal
matches. The matrix, rails, and voyage are faces of one mapping workspace, not
separate applications. Misordering remains a property of the recalled route,
and omission remains a source-side absence; neither is fabricated as
participant text.

Source and recall remain separately compiled evidence-bearing processes. A
match-checked join can connect them, but identity proof does not grant
permission to reveal recall text. Story and recall may share focus while
retaining independent grains, horizons, and privacy policies.

Autobiographical recall belongs in the same instrument shell but is a distinct
scientific packet. When no observed event exists, the interview transcript is
the observational text. Interviewer cues, probes, speakers, and phases remain
visible as protocol, never as participant memory content. The induced episode
appears only as an explicitly hypothesized or structurally derived projection,
not as a fabricated source. `MemoryAddress` expresses target episodes, other
episodes, personal or general knowledge, discourse, and unresolved material;
traditional internal/external scores are declared overlays. Phenomenology and
reported confidence remain separate from event membership, truth, and
veridicality.

## What excellence means

An excellent StoryAtlas should be immediately orienting, visually calm, and
scientifically exact. Beauty here comes from information hierarchy, readable
typography, meaningful motion, restrained channels, and progressive
disclosure—not from obscuring uncertainty.

Success means that a researcher can:

- identify the current region, projection, scale, and evidence horizon at a
  glance;
- reach the evidence for any scientific mark and its derivation in at most two
  deliberate actions;
- distinguish scene changes, temporal jumps, flashbacks, reports, uncertainty,
  omissions, blends, role or polarity errors, and external material;
- inspect raw and aggregated feature signals without hidden smoothing;
- traverse a 100,000-word work without losing selection or auditability;
- reproduce a saved view from model identity, semantic state, view
  specification, feature receipts, and layout receipts;
- use the complete instrument through keyboard, screen-reader, textual-twin,
  reduced-motion, and colour-independent paths.

The present War-of-the-Ghosts edition is an important proof of integrity: exact
DOM text, deterministic pagination, renderer overlays, shared semantic
selection, an epistemic horizon, textual twins, and receipts. It is not yet the
destination. The vision is fulfilled only when those guarantees survive richer
projections, real semantic zoom, numerical features, source–recall mapping,
autobiographical interviews, population views, and novel scale.
