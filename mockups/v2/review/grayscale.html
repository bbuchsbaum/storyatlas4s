Greyscale and colour-vision check

The same component specimen under four transforms. Simulations are computed with the Viénot–Brettel–Mollon dichromat projection in linear sRGB, and greyscale is relative luminance re-encoded — not a CSS filter, so the hex values here are the ones a reader actually sees.

The result, stated plainly

The two packet inks are equiluminant by construction — 1.03:1 apart as authored, and never more than 1.40:1 apart under any dichromat simulation. That is not a defect: they are not meant to be told apart by hue. Role is carried by the 3px edge and the written label.

New under the relaxed rule: the column-mass ramp stays ordered without hue — #E6E6E6 → #CFCFCF → #B3B3B3 → #919191 → #4B4B4B. It reinforces bar height rather than replacing it — and zero stays categorically distinct because it is hatched, not pale. That is exactly what “never colour alone” permits and “colour never carries meaning” forbade.

How to re-run this Take every token pair the design relies on; project through the dichromat planes; recompute WCAG relative-luminance contrast on the projected pair. A pair that only passes as authored has a colour dependency hiding in it. This check belongs in CI beside the existing static-edition laws, not in a designer's eyeball.
