SYNTHETIC PLACEHOLDER — NOT SOURCE OR MODEL OUTPUT
StoryAtlas mockup v2 · deliverables 1 and 13
Concept statement and annotation notes

One reading surface, one question at a time, one inspector you can close. The exact words never leave the screen, so the expensive step in an evidence check — getting back to the text — costs nothing.

1 · The information hierarchy, and what each tier is allowed to cost

The reference sketch put six instruments at equal volume. Reviewers split on it: the composition captured the product, but nothing in it told you where to look. The resolution is not fewer capabilities — it is a declared rank, with a budget attached to each tier.

tier 1 · permanent

The exact text

Real DOM text, selectable and searchable, in the largest type on the screen. It is never summarised, never restyled to carry a claim, and never scrolled away to make room.

Budget: 648px, about 44% of the working width, at every viewport above the reflow breakpoint.

tier 2 · one at a time

The current question

Exactly one primary projection, replaceable in one click, plus at most one docked quantitative feature. Changing it changes the question, not the object under inspection.

Budget: the remaining width, and one feature track. A second projection is a replacement, never an addition.

tier 3 · collapsible

The evidence check

The inspector. Field-addressable, never a summary. It is not a permanently equal third panel — it collapses to a 40px rail and the projection takes the space.

Budget: 296px open, 40px closed. Its content survives the collapse.

tier 4 · quiet edges

Controls and contracts

Scale, feature, horizon, export, and the printed projection contract. Present, legible, never competing for attention with the text or the figure.

Budget: the top and bottom edges. Nothing in tier 4 uses saturated colour or type above 13px.

The annotation budget is part of the hierarchy. Seven annotation channels exist — feature, hierarchy, entity, relation, context, claim, recall. Three are painted by default: one hierarchy layer, one context layer, one feature. The other four are one click away and are named in the Codex header so their absence is visible rather than silent. Dimming is never used to say “unimportant”; it means one thing only, and it is labelled.

2 · The interaction model: one loop, five steps, no dead ends

The loop is the one the vision names. What the design contributes is the cost of each step — and the guarantee that step 5 never undoes steps 1 to 4.

1

Notice

A 62px overview strip carries structure, viewport and selection — and nothing else.

cost: no click
›
2

Select

From any face. One typed Set[Address] shared by every visible view.

cost: 1 click or Enter
›
3

Reach the words

The Codex never left. The supporting span is already on screen, already highlighted.

cost: zero — this is the point
›
4

Inspect

Provenance, alternatives, reducer, coverage, receipt — each its own field, none bundled.

cost: 1 action, or 0 if open
›
5

Change scale or view

Focus survives. Placement becomes on-mark, via-ancestor or off-projection — declared, never dropped.

cost: 1 click · loop returns to 1

Three semantic axes are kept apart because conflating them is the most common way an instrument like this lies: narrative level (Story · Episode · Scene · Event), surface detail (Hidden · Sentences · Tokens) and feature window (9w · 25w · 61w · by scene). They sit in three separately labelled control groups, divided by rules, and paging through text is never labelled as a change of level.

3 · What changed from the reference sketch, and why

In the sketch In v2 Because
Six panels at equal weight, each with its own legend and controls. One Codex, one primary projection, one collapsible inspector, quiet edges. The chief reviewer was right that the composition captured the product, and the UX reviewers were right that it could not be the default. The broad view survives as an analyst overview; the workspace is task-centred.
Prose, sentence numbers and word offsets presented as The War of the Ghosts. A named synthetic fixture with its own address namespace, watermarked on every artboard. None of it could round-trip to the admitted source. The source text is not in this repository by design, and inventing a stand-in for it is the one thing a design about evidence must not do.
“Confidence: 0.89” over a bundle of unrelated claims. Per-field quantities, each with estimand, reducer, window, coverage, provider, receipt — and “no calibration model”. The number looked like a calibrated probability and was neither calibrated nor about one claim. Two separate defects, so two separate fixes.
A force-directed graph with “Layout: Chronological” and no declared axis. A printed contract strip on every projection, including a “not encoded” line. Position, distance and layout carried no stated meaning, so readers would supply one. The Atlas twin already emits this contract; the header now shows it.
Node colour as the primary type encoding. Row position, fill texture, border style and a written label. Hue is redundant. ADR 0002 V-U5. The categorical space is open-ended and the textual twin cannot represent a hue, so the twin would stop being a faithful oracle.
A single “External / Irrelevant” alignment bin. Association, commentary, unsupported intrusion, source-consistent inference and uninterpretable — five scored columns — with unranked held apart from all of them. A licensed inference and an unsupported intrusion have opposite scientific meanings. Collapsing them discards the distinction the study exists to measure.
“[skipped]” inserted into the participant recall rail. A source-side column gauge with a declared denominator. Omission is missing mass on the source axis. Writing it into the recall transcript fabricates participant speech.
Misordering drawn as a per-cell alignment class. A transition overlay over consecutive source-addressed units, with its denominator printed. One cell cannot be out of order. Order is a property of the edge between two cells, and its denominator excludes off-source and abstained units.
Recall shown only as nearest-neighbour arrows between two rails. A posterior matrix first; rails and route are secondary, on demand. Arrows can only show a winner. The matrix retains hierarchy, multimodality, abstention and every off-source destination, and each row visibly sums to one.

4 · Annotation notes — every non-obvious encoding

Deliverable 13. If an element is not in this table, it carries no meaning and may be restyled freely. The right-hand column is the load-bearing one: a channel that does not declare what it excludes will be over-read.

Element Channel Means exactly Does not mean
Codex line type Exact source text, monospace 16/24.8, real selectable DOM text. Not a rendering of the text — it is the text, and it hashes to the source receipt.
Gutter rule, solid position A scene boundary begins on this line. Not an event boundary; events are marks, not rules.
Gutter rule, dashed line style This line sits in a reported or embedded context (lane 1). Not doubt about the text. The frame is explicit; only its content is unasserted.
Atlas x position position Exact discourse offset in UTF-16 code units, linear and to scale. Not story-world time, not reading time, not duration.
Atlas y lane position Context lane. Narrated world is lane 0. Vertical distance has no metric. Lane 3 is not “further” than lane 1.
Atlas region area The hull of the exact support of a segment’s visible children. Not importance, not duration, not confidence.
Thread, solid 1.5px line style One entity’s participations, connected in discourse order. Asserts nothing about the interval between two participations.
Portal, dashed 6/4 line style A stored reference edge whose endpoints are not discourse-adjacent. Not causation, and not a temporal ordering.
Loom left column position Exact discourse order, total. Not a time axis; the spacing is uniform by construction.
Loom right column position Rank in the compiled story-world partial order. Not a clock. Equal spacing implies no equal intervals.
Loom shared band shape Situations the model does not order relative to each other. Not simultaneity. Incomparable is not the same as concurrent.
Loom slope line The same situation in both orders. A crossing is a temporal discontinuity. Slope angle has no magnitude; a steeper line is not a bigger jump.
Feature line weight weight One declared rolling window: 9w thin dashed, 25w medium solid, 61w broad. Not a confidence band, and not a smoothing the view invented.
Feature hatch texture The provider returned no value over this extent. Not zero, not low, and never interpolated across.
Matrix cell area area Posterior mass on that destination. Row totals are exactly 1.00. Not a rank, not a similarity score, not a p-value.
Matrix vertical texture texture An off-source destination, one of five named kinds. Not an error and not “irrelevant”.
Matrix dashed median line style Unranked or abstained mass, held rather than redistributed. Not zero and not missing.
Dashed panel border border style Hypothesised or structurally derived — no independent observation exists. Not low confidence. It is a different kind of object, not a weaker one.
2px alarm border weight A build, receipt or authorisation problem that blocks reading the value. Not a warning about the science; it is a warning about the artefact.
Blue focus ring colour + outline Current keyboard focus. The only saturated colour in the interface. Not selection. Selection is the dashed outline and persists without focus.

One rule covers the whole table

Position and area are measured; texture and border style are categorical; hue is redundant. That ordering is deliberate, and it is why the greyscale plate is not a degraded version of this design — it is the same design with a decorative layer removed.
