StoryAtlas mockup v2 · colour direction
Four directions for the same instrument

Same fragment, same geometry, same type — only the palette changes. D is the dark answer, shown as a peer so it competes fairly rather than as an afterthought. Two moves are common to all three and are the real fix: the pastel washes are gone (selection is an 8% tint of the accent, not a coloured fill), and the watermark stops being the loudest colour on screen.

Every ratio below is computed against that direction's own paper, and every accent is re-checked after dichromat simulation. Because colour carries no meaning here, the only hard floor is the focus ring at 3:1 — but headroom differs a lot between these three, and that is a real reason to prefer one.

Why you'd pick it. The one that actually looks like a made object. Rag paper, warm black, and a printer’s vermilion used the way a rubric is used in a printed edition — the reader’s mark, and nothing else.

What it costs. Cream can read as dingy on a poor panel, and the vermilion falls to 3.34:1 under tritanopia — it clears the 3:1 non-text floor with no headroom. Red also reads as “error” to software-trained eyes.

What it costs. The texture vocabulary is the cost, not the colour. Hatch, stipple and cross-hatch are this design’s load-bearing non-colour layer, and light-on-dark strokes bloom — see the panel below. Every one needs re-tuning, and PDF plates still export light.

Why you'd pick it. Warm paper without going to cream, plus two deep inks with real saturation used as edges and marks rather than fills. The indigo focus ring has the most headroom of the three by a wide margin.

What it costs. The least daring of the three. It is a disciplined palette rather than a striking one, and it asks the layout and the type to do the distinguishing.

What a dark theme actually costs

Not the palette. The textures — which in this design are the layer that carries meaning, because colour is forbidden from doing it.

A light stroke on a dark ground reads thicker than a dark stroke of the same width on a light ground. At the light theme's 1.3px the hatch fills in and the stipple turns to noise; the four textures stop being four.

Re-tuned at ~0.75× width, widened pitch, and stepped back from the text tone to the muted tone, they separate again — but that is a second set of values to specify, test and keep in sync, not a swap.

Where it lands in the code

Not a stylesheet change. The graphic params are compile-time constants in intaglio/…/style.scala:30–53 — rgba(120,120,120,1.0), rgba(160,160,160,0.30), and Rgba.Black for every stroke, thread, portal and label.

Those same params produce the static edition and the publication plates, so a theme is not a viewer preference — it becomes part of the export receipt. The shell's hardcoded blacks (app/index.html:11,28,32,33,37) need the same treatment, inline, since no external stylesheet is permitted.

PDF plates export light regardless — §11 forbids flood fills that drink ink — so the light theme has to exist either way.

What changes in all three

These are the fixes for the actual complaint, and they are independent of which palette wins:

WashesDeleted. Selection becomes an 8% tint of the accent plus the dashed outline that already carries it — 1.07:1 against paper, so it reads as a lit row, not a coloured one. Grey rampFive near-identical warm greys cut to three with real separation between them. WatermarkNo longer amber. It stays dominant through weight and position, not saturation. Role tintsGone as fills. Source / recall / hypothesised become a 3px left edge and a word — which is all they were ever allowed to be.

My recommendation

C · Oxide for the default, D · Nocturne as a real second theme. It gets the edition warmth without committing to cream, and its focus ring is the only one of the three with genuine headroom — 8.00:1 as authored and never below 7.69:1 under any dichromat simulation, against 3.34:1 for the vermilion.

On dark: worth building, but as the second theme rather than the default. This is a reading instrument whose tier-1 surface is prose held for long stretches, and whose named deliverables are printable plates — both point light-first. D is a genuine option, not a courtesy.

That defect is fixed: metadata grey was 4.29:1 on tinted grounds, under the 4.5 floor. It is now #69625C at 5.52:1 on paper and 4.61:1 on the deepest surface.
